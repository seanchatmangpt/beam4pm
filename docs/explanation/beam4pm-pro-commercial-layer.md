# beam4pm_pro: A Commercial Layer, Not a Fork

`beam4pm_pro` is not a separate product with its own event model. It is a set of Elixir
modules — `lib/beam4pm_pro_*.ex` — that sit on top of the same `bpm:RecordType` records the
open substrate generates, and answer questions the open substrate deliberately does not:
who is allowed to do this, is this deployment topology actually compatible, what happens
when a real actuation is refused, and what does one object type's interaction with another
actually look like. None of these questions have a natural place in an ontology-generated
record projection, which is why they live in a separate, hand-written layer rather than
folded into `ontology.ttl`.

## Why a separate layer, not a ggen extension

The generated core (`src/`, `lib/beam4pm_*.ex` produced by `ggen sync run`, see
`/Users/sac/beam4pm/CLAUDE.md`) is a pure function of `ontology.ttl` plus the vendored pack's
templates — 31 record types, four language projections, and a fixed set of discovery/codec
operations. That determinism is the whole point: `scripts/gate_m2_check.sh` proves it by
deleting every `GENERATED by ggen` file and regenerating byte-for-byte. Licensing, tenancy,
billing reconciliation, and actuation admission are not properties of an event log's shape —
they are properties of a *deployment*, and they change independently of the ontology.
Putting them in `ontology.ttl` would make the substrate's determinism proof depend on
concerns (a customer's license key, a tenant's role assignments) that have nothing to do
with what a `dfg_edge` or an `ocel_event` is. Keeping them in `lib/beam4pm_pro_*.ex` — hand-
authored, not ggen output — means the manufacturing pipeline stays a pure function of the
ontology, and the commercial concerns stay auditable as ordinary hand-written Elixir with
their own tests, independent of any ontology regeneration.

## Three capability tiers

### Analytical: what the process actually did

This tier answers questions about a single case's shape and a single deployment's health,
without inventing new process-mining primitives beyond what `beam4pm_discovery` already
computes. `BeamPM.ProcessGovernor` (`lib/beam4pm_process_governor.ex:1-77`) sequences named
actuations against a graph-declared contract with exact-state-hash fencing, and its
`replay/2` (`:492`) reconstructs a `LogTrace.t()` and `dfg` from a process's own receipts —
turning operational history back into the same discovery primitives the open substrate
already exposes, rather than a parallel analytics engine. `BeamPM.Pro.Doctor`
(`lib/beam4pm_pro_doctor.ex:1-17`) runs an injectable check suite (version, migration,
connector, entitlement, provenance) and produces a redacted, JSON-safe support bundle via
`bundle/1` (`:51`) — the operational-health counterpart to discovery's log-health.
`BeamPM.Pro.Compatibility` (`lib/beam4pm_pro_compatibility.ex:1-10`) uses real
`Version.match?/2` semver checks in `check/1` (`:34`) to refuse incompatible component
pairings before they cause a harder-to-diagnose failure downstream.

### Object-centric: relationships the four-language core does not model

`beam4pm_discovery`'s DFG and trace primitives operate on single-case-notion event logs.
`BeamPM.Pro.OcpmDiscovery` (`lib/beam4pm_pro_ocpm_discovery.ex:1-27`) works one level up, over
`ocel_event`/`ocel_object` maps directly: `object_type_interactions/2` (`:44`) counts how
often pairs of object types co-occur in the same event, and
`object_type_activity_frequency/4` (`:67`) breaks activity frequency down per object type.
Neither function synthesizes an object-centric Petri net or detects convergence/divergence —
the module's own `gaps/0` (`:87`) names exactly what is not implemented:
`object_centric_petri_net_synthesis`, `divergence_free_log_transformation`,
`multi_instance_dfg`, `convergence_detection`. Declaring the gap in code that ships, rather
than only in a doc, is the same discipline the manufacturing pipeline applies to its own
scope: state what a component does and does not do, in the artifact itself.
`BeamPM.Pro.Simulation` (`lib/beam4pm_pro_simulation.ex:1-17`) is adjacent but distinct — it
performs structural what-if analysis over an already-discovered DFG (`apply_change/2` at
`:29`/`:33`, `reachable_from/2` at `:47` via real BFS, `has_cycle?/1` at `:70` via real DFS),
not discrete-event or throughput simulation; its own `gaps/0` (`:119`) makes the same
distinction explicit.

### Prescriptive: closing the loop into a real subprocess

The furthest tier from "read the log" is "act on the world and prove it happened."
`BeamPM.Actuation` (`lib/beam4pm_actuation.ex:205-267`) is a BRCE (validate → admit →
execute → observe + receipt) pipeline over a real subprocess gym bridge — not a mocked
executor. `GymBridge.open/2` (`:25`) and `GymBridge.request/3` (`:52`) talk to an actual
`priv/bridges/*.py` process; `Actuation.run/2` (`:548`) requires real `:gym` and `:bridge`
options and returns `{:ok, %{receipt_path: ..., performed: true, ...}}` only after a real
execution, writing a `beam4pm-brce/v1` receipt to disk. `Actuation.admission_verdict/1`
(`:574`) enforces a graph-derived allowlist before anything executes — admission is checked
against `admitted_actuations/0` (`:596`), not against caller-supplied trust.
`BeamPM.ProcessGovernor` builds multi-step contracts on top of this without reimplementing
admission or execution itself (`lib/beam4pm_process_governor.ex:1-77`), and
`BeamPM.ReceiptChain` (`lib/beam4pm_receipt_chain.ex:1-92`) adds an opt-in hash chain over
those receipts' raw on-disk bytes — `hash_file!/1` (`:132`) hashes what is actually on disk,
and `verify/2` (`:172`) walks the chain to detect breakage, rather than trusting an in-memory
audit log that could diverge from the files it describes.

## The commercial substrate underneath the tiers

None of the three tiers are usable in a multi-tenant, paid deployment without answering
"who," "which version," and "did they pay for it" — and those three questions get their own
modules rather than being scattered as ad hoc checks inside each capability.
`BeamPM.Pro.Tenancy` (`lib/beam4pm_pro_tenancy.ex:1-27`) is fail-closed: `authorize/3`
(`:59`) checks the tenant boundary before the role capability, so a request against the
wrong tenant is refused before role logic even runs — deliberately not adopting Triplex
given the ETS-based data layer (`:1-27`). `BeamPM.Pro.License`
(`lib/beam4pm_pro_license.ex:1-15`) is a struct with HMAC-SHA256 `sign/2` (`:51`) and
`verify/2` (`:64`), returning `{:error, :tampered}` or `{:error, :offline_unverifiable}` as
distinct outcomes rather than a single boolean — and it is deliberately kept separate from
entitlement/actuation authority: holding a valid license is not the same claim as being
entitled to a specific capability. `BeamPM.Pro.CapabilityManifest`
(`lib/beam4pm_pro_capability_manifest.ex:1-16`) closes the loop on all of the above by
introspecting real repo state and refusing to let a claimed capability status
(`verify/1` at `:68`) diverge from what `evidence_for/1` (`:53`) can actually show — the
commercial layer applying the same no-overclaiming discipline to itself that the
open substrate's generation gates apply to generated code.

Underneath all three tiers, `beam4pm_billing` (`src/beam4pm_billing.erl:1-49`, note: `src/`,
not `lib/`) and `beam4pm_entitlement` (`src/beam4pm_entitlement.erl:1-58`) already exist as
real, hand-designed reconciliation logic with matching eunit and ExUnit tests —
`reconcile_billing/4` (`:257`) dedups usage events by `event_id` over a half-open period
window, and `reconcile_entitlement/2` (`:294`) is an idempotent, order-independent watermark
fold over redelivered or out-of-order lifecycle events. These two modules are the load-
bearing evidence that `beam4pm_pro`'s commercial reconciliation is not aspirational: MP3
(entitlement) and MP6 (billing) reconciliation are implemented and tested today, ahead of the
marketplace and pricing tooling that would consume them.

## Why this stays a layer, not a merge

Folding `beam4pm_pro` into the generated core would either force licensing and tenancy
concepts into `ontology.ttl` — coupling the substrate's determinism proof to commercial
state that has nothing to do with process-mining semantics — or force the pro modules
through the ggen pipeline, where their job (authorization decisions, HMAC verification,
subprocess actuation) has no natural RDF/SHACL shape to generate from. Keeping the boundary
at the `lib/beam4pm_pro_*.ex` / ggen-generated-`lib/beam4pm_*.ex` split means the open
substrate's manufacturing guarantee (`scripts/gate_m2_check.sh`, a pure function of ontology
plus templates) stays intact regardless of what the commercial layer does, while the
commercial layer stays free to depend on things — a customer's HMAC key, a tenant's role
table, a subprocess gym bridge — that a deterministic code generator has no business knowing
about.

# TAI Quality and Contract Operations Applied to beam4pm — v26.8.29

## Purpose

Apply the operating discipline associated with Technology Applications Inc. (TAI) to beam4pm/beam4pm_pro as a modern software-product and cloud-marketplace company.

The relevant pattern is not nostalgia or branding. It is mission-oriented contract execution: customer mission first, Total Quality Leadership, full lifecycle responsibility, configuration/document control, validation/verification, standards fluency, supportability, continuous improvement, disciplined operations and evidence that every contract obligation was actually completed.

## Operating doctrine

### Mission before artifact

The unit of success is the customer's mission outcome, not a feature, commit, dashboard or listing.

For beam4pm_pro:

`customer process problem -> admitted environment -> process evidence -> actionable understanding -> verified outcome -> lifecycle support`

A technically impressive deployment that fails the customer's mission is not successful.

### Total Quality Leadership

Quality is not a test phase. It is the operating system connecting requirements, design/manufacture, verification, support, customer acceptance and continuous improvement.

Every defect should answer:

- Which requirement/invariant was violated?
- Which transition failed?
- Why did admission/manufacturing/verification not catch it earlier?
- What permanent guard makes recurrence harder?
- What customer/contract evidence must be corrected?

### Full lifecycle ownership

beam4pm_pro must own the product lifecycle from presale admission through retirement:

`requirements -> architecture -> manufacture -> qualification -> marketplace publication -> entitlement -> deployment -> training/onboarding -> operation -> support -> update -> renewal -> migration/retirement`

Do not optimize launch at the expense of lifecycle supportability.

## Contract mission package

Every enterprise contract/private offer should have a corresponding internal mission package containing:

- customer mission statement;
- process/use-case scope;
- environment boundaries;
- authority/access boundaries;
- commercial identities and term;
- acceptance criteria;
- deliverables;
- support level/severity definitions;
- security/data-boundary commitments;
- configuration baseline;
- change-control policy;
- evidence/receipt obligations;
- renewal/transition plan.

This internal packet remains aligned with, but does not replace, legally binding customer documents.

## Configuration management

Maintain exact identity across:

- canonical ontology/specification version;
- ggen/generator/packs;
- generated source tree;
- dependencies/toolchain;
- container/package digest;
- SBOM/provenance;
- marketplace listing/plan version;
- entitlement adapter version;
- deployment configuration;
- customer environment scope;
- process-model schema;
- support/runbook revision.

A support engineer must be able to answer, “What exact system is this customer running?” without inference from memory.

## Verification and validation

### Verification

Did we manufacture the specified system correctly?

Examples:

- deterministic regeneration;
- compile/type/property tests;
- cross-language semantic round-trip;
- marketplace API contract tests;
- air-gap/no-egress tests;
- supply-chain/provenance checks.

### Validation

Does the system perform the customer's real mission?

Examples:

- deploy into representative customer topology;
- reconstruct a known process;
- identify a seeded/real bottleneck;
- operate during telemetry/marketplace/API failure;
- support an incident without hidden vendor access;
- renew/upgrade entitlement without service loss.

Neither substitutes for the other.

## Supportability

Design for support before scale.

Every deployment should expose a local diagnostic bundle that can be generated by the customer and reviewed without direct vendor access to sensitive telemetry.

Diagnostic evidence should include:

- exact product/config identity;
- health/standing;
- entitlement state;
- ingestion adapter state;
- queue/backlog/WIP;
- storage state;
- process-inference pipeline status;
- resource pressure;
- recent bounded errors;
- receipt verification;
- redacted topology metadata where customer permits.

## Severity model

Suggested starting point:

### SEV-1 Mission unavailable

Production process-intelligence service unavailable or causing unacceptable production impact; air-gap/security boundary violation; marketplace entitlement defect causing broad service denial.

### SEV-2 Major degradation

Material process coverage/inference unavailable, substantial data delay, failed upgrade without total outage.

### SEV-3 Functional defect

Bounded feature/integration defect with workaround.

### SEV-4 Question/enhancement

Documentation, advisory or non-urgent improvement.

Actual contractual response targets remain `UNKNOWN` until support packaging/economics are admitted.

## Change control

Customer production change is not “push latest.”

`change request -> impact analysis -> admitted release -> customer/environment compatibility -> authority -> deployment -> verification -> receipt -> rollback readiness`

For air-gapped customers, import/export custody and signatures are part of change control.

## Continuous improvement

Each incident, support request, proof failure, lost deal, security objection and renewal objection becomes structured input.

Classify by failed operating transition:

- product capability;
- manufacturing/ggen;
- marketplace integration;
- deployment;
- security/compliance;
- documentation;
- support;
- pricing/packaging;
- qualification/sales;
- customer value hypothesis.

Then repair the process, not merely the instance.

## Definition of contract success

A beam4pm_pro contract is complete only when:

- promised capability is deployed and accepted;
- exact configuration is known;
- customer operators can use/support it;
- required documentation/evidence is delivered;
- billing/entitlement reconciles;
- security/data boundaries are maintained;
- support obligations are met;
- known limitations are disclosed;
- renewal/transition is handled without abandonment.

That is the modern equivalent of “complete the contract on time with no hidden quality debt.”
